Frequency comparisons are withheld. A week's traffic measured over a couple of observed days is not a small sample, it is a different quantity: divide it by a seven-day baseline and every carrier looks cut in half by our own downtime. The observed counts are still shown, the bars are left empty rather than drawn against a ratio the window cannot support, and every carrier reads UNKNOWN until enough days accumulate.

Stopped operations

Carriers that have gone silent for longer than the threshold for their scope, dated from their last observed flight. Silent days during a coverage outage are not counted.

Back in the air

Services observed operating again after a stop, in the last 30 days.

Carriers

Weekly departures against a frozen pre-escalation baseline. Traffic is scaled to the days we actually observed before it is compared, so the left figure is a full week only when enough of the week was seen — otherwise it is the raw count, labelled with the days behind it, and no bar is drawn. A carrier only reads SUSPENDED after three consecutive observed silent days; blind days neither confirm a suspension nor clear one.

API
  • v1/status.json — every carrier, current vs baseline
  • v1/suspensions.json — who stopped, when, for how long, and who came back
  • v1/alerts.json — suspended and reduced routes
  • v1/airlines/{ICAO}.json — one carrier, route by route
  • v1/airports/{ICAO}.json — one airport, who is still serving it
  • v1/firs.json — overflight counts per FIR
  • v1/advisories.json — EASA CZIB state
  • v1/health.json — data span, last runs, baseline readiness
